# Bike2Beans MVP Product Prioritization

## 1. User Problem Definition
Urban bike commuters who already buy coffee on weekday mornings face fragmented routines: route planning in one app, rewards in another, and no direct incentive to choose bike-friendly cafes. They need a single lightweight flow that helps them discover partner cafes on their commute and earn rewards tied to bike trips.

Primary target users: weekday bike commuters (ages 22-45) in dense neighborhoods, and independent cafes seeking repeat morning traffic.

Expected measurable outcome: at least 30% of new commuter signups complete one bike-trip check-in and one cafe purchase in their first 7 days.

## 2. Strategic Importance
This problem sits at the intersection of two high-frequency habits (commuting and coffee), creating a strong daily retention opportunity. Solving it creates a differentiated network effect: more commuters increase partner-cafe value, and more cafes increase commuter utility.

Primary target users: commuters with 3+ weekly bike commute days, and cafes with high morning throughput but weak loyalty program performance.

Expected measurable outcome: weekly active commuter retention above 35% by week 8 in launch neighborhoods, plus at least 15% lift in repeat morning transactions at pilot cafes.

## 3. MVP Recommendation
Prioritize a narrow, testable MVP with four capabilities:
1. Rider onboarding with commute window and preferred neighborhoods.
2. Partner cafe map/list filtered by route relevance.
3. Simple trip check-in + purchase verification (single QR flow at cafe).
4. Points-to-reward wallet (one clear reward tier for launch).

Primary target users: first-time commuters evaluating utility quickly, and pilot cafes needing low-friction staff workflows.

Expected measurable outcome: median time from signup to first verified reward-eligible purchase under 3 days, and cafe staff verification completion success above 90% during peak hours.

## 4. Long-Term Considerations
After MVP signal is validated, sequence growth into:
1. Route-aware recommendations (weather/time-aware).
2. Team and employer challenges for B2B2C distribution.
3. Tiered rewards and subscription perks for high-frequency riders.
4. Multi-city partner onboarding playbook.

Primary target users: high-frequency commuters, operations managers at small cafe groups, and HR/wellness leads at commuter-heavy employers.

Expected measurable outcome: increase monthly rides-to-purchase conversion by 20% post-recommendation launch, and secure at least 3 employer pilots with 100+ eligible riders each.

## 5. Monetization Path
Phase monetization to protect early adoption:
1. Phase 1: cafe partner SaaS fee for loyalty tooling (flat monthly pilot fee).
2. Phase 2: transaction-linked fee for verified reward redemptions.
3. Phase 3: commuter premium plan (enhanced rewards, challenge boosts, partner perks).

Primary target users: independent cafes (phase 1), regional cafe groups (phase 2), and super-commuters riding 4+ days/week (phase 3).

Expected measurable outcome: reach positive unit contribution per active cafe within 90 days of onboarding, and achieve at least 8% premium conversion among riders with 12+ monthly trips.

## 6. Metrics and Decision Checkpoints
Track one decision stack from acquisition to monetization:
- Acquisition: commuter signup-to-first-check-in rate.
- Activation: first check-in to first verified purchase rate.
- Retention: week-4 commuter activity rate.
- Supply health: active cafes per neighborhood and verification reliability.
- Revenue: average revenue per active cafe and premium conversion.

Decision checkpoints:
1. End of month 1: continue pilot only if signup-to-first-verified-purchase is at least 25%.
2. End of month 2: expand neighborhood coverage only if week-4 retention is at least 30% and cafe verification success is at least 90%.
3. End of month 3: begin phase-1 monetization only if repeat purchase frequency is at least 1.8 per active commuter per month.

Primary target users: product leadership, growth team, and cafe partner success managers.

Expected measurable outcome: clear go/no-go decisions at each checkpoint with quantified thresholds, reducing roadmap ambiguity and preventing premature expansion.
